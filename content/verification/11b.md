# Verification, batch 11b

All 29 sources were re-fetched and every quote was found (validate-content and check-sources pass). Corrections below are about whether the text goes beyond what the sources say.

## chlorpromazine: corrected
- Body and myth said US state hospitals were "already shrinking" in 1955. Braslow says only that the 1955 peak was followed by discharge of most patients in 1970-80, and 1955 is the year after Thorazine's approval, so "already falling" was unsupported. Rewrote body, myth record and confidence note to say the peak was in 1955, the year after approval, and most patients left in 1970-80.
- Braslow's point was that the drugs "had little overall effect" on how psychiatrists understood and treated psychosis; the body's "at first changed little" was not in the source. Reworded.
- "Their papers sent it around the world" overreached. Ban says the papers "set the stage for the introduction" of the drug. Softened. Added the 24 March 1952 start of the Sainte-Anne trials (Ban).
- Summary: "first modern antipsychotic" changed to "first widely used".
- Image: the file is a 2024 photograph of a modern Algerian-market Largactil bottle, not period material. Caption now says so. A better period image was not found.
- Residual doubt: the ranking of causes of deinstitutionalization is a historians' judgment; the sources support "one cause among several" but do not quantify it.

## intensive-care: corrected
- Ibsen/Ángeles-Sistac gives two locations for the "first multidisciplinary ICU", Blegdam (abstract) and the Municipal Hospital from 1 August 1953 (text). The body stated Municipal Hospital as fact. Reworded to "moved to the Municipal Hospital, where he set up what is often called the first ICU" and the confidence note now mentions the disagreement.
- Other disagreements (1,000 vs 1,500 students; mortality 87% to 40% vs under 15%) were already in the note.
- Residual doubt: the exact dating and location of the "first ICU" remain contested.

## dna-double-helix: corrected
- Body said "Franklin was precise about its dimensions and symmetry". No source says this, and the NLM page says she doubted the A form was helical. Replaced with the sourced fact that she and Gosling found two forms, A and B.
- Myth record said Gosling passed on the photograph "with Franklin's knowledge" as if settled. Cobb and Comfort report Gosling's claim, but the NLM account says Wilkins showed it "without Franklin's knowledge". The text now states both. "Franklin's own work had taken her far toward the structure" was vague; replaced with the NLM statements that she never argued the B form was non-helical and drafted her paper before seeing the Watson-Crick model. Also added that Perutz's report was as important as the photograph (Cobb and Comfort).
- Summary: "came from four scientists, not two" stated a contested 2023 argument as fact. Reworded.
- Image checked: Science Museum thymine template, CC BY-SA 2.0, described as part of Crick and Watson's model. Caption confirmed.
- Residual doubt: whether Franklin was an "equal member" of a quartet is Cobb and Comfort's argument. The body attributes it to them.

## heart-lung-machine: corrected
- "In 1955 the world's open-heart operations were all being done in Minnesota" is Austin's statement, but Passaroni records a first on-pump open-heart operation in São Paulo in October 1955 and Austin himself notes Cleveland beginning by the end of 1955. Reworded to "nearly all ... though by year's end surgeons in Cleveland, São Paulo and elsewhere had begun".
- "Had made open-heart surgery routine" within a few years softened to "soon helped make ... routine" (Leivaditis).
- Added image: Wellcome photograph (CC BY 4.0) of a London heart-lung machine with a disc oxygenator, c. 1955-60, built by Denis Melrose. The caption says it is not Gibbon's design.
- Residual doubt: the Gibbon patient's name (Cecelia Bavolek) is not in any source read, so it is omitted. Clarence Dennis's earlier failed 1951 bypass operation (Leivaditis) is not mentioned.

## kidney-transplant: corrected
- "Kidneys had been transplanted before, and all had failed." The 1945 Brigham arm graft bridged a woman until her own kidneys recovered, and Merrill reports one of Hume's nine thigh grafts lasting months. Changed to "none had worked for more than a few months", and the confidence note was changed to match.
- All other facts were confirmed in the Murray lecture, Merrill/Abdi, Koch/Tullius and the Murray biography (Herrick dates, Murray's death 26 Nov 2012, fingerprints at a police station, clergy consultation, April 1962 unrelated-donor graft).
- No image added; Commons searches were rate-limited. Tier 1 therefore has no image.
- Residual doubt: "first successful organ transplant" depends on defining success as lasting function.

## polio-vaccine: corrected
- Summary said the Cutter batch "paralysed hundreds"; the cited figure is 192 paralytic cases. Changed to "nearly 200" (American spelling "paralyzed").
- The Murrow exchange: the source dates it only "1955 (April 12)", on CBS's *See It Now*. The body's "That day ... asked Salk" now reads "Asked on CBS television ... Salk is recorded as answering".
- "Made it possible to produce virus in bulk" is not in any cited source; cut. The Cutter ruling's effect now says "one historian notes ... led some drug companies to stop making vaccine" (Hedley-Whyte).
- Image added: the March of Dimes photograph of newspaper headlines about the polio vaccine tests (public domain, dated 13 April 1955 on Commons). The caption states only what the file page says.
- Residual doubt: the 77 million Soviet figure counts only the USSR (Sabin source); Hedley-Whyte gives other, higher totals.

## ultrasound: corrected (minor)
- "Later named the Diasonograph" is not in the sources. Changed to "the Diasonograph". Other claims (1955 start, borrowed flaw detector, bistable images, earlier Edler/Hertz and Wild work) confirmed.
- Residual doubt: none beyond the "first" being limited to the fetus and pelvis, as the note says.

## pacemaker: confirmed
- All facts match Aquilina and McIlraith (evening of 8 October 1958, 43-year-old, 20-30 attacks daily, 8-hour first device, about one week for the second, 22 generators, Chardack June 1960). Only change: summary "told a surgeon" to "pleaded with", closer to the source wording.
- Residual doubt: the source also calls Larsson's device the first rechargeable pacemaker, so "self-powered" for Chardack's device is a distinction the body leaves unexplained.
