# Verification report, batch 03b

## mihintale-hospital: corrected
- Body said "excavators uncovered a monastery hospital" at Mihintale. The Mihintale catalog record describes only a ground plan; excavation is mentioned only for the Polonnaruwa hospital. Reworded to ground plan/ruins; the confidence note no longer says excavation identified it.
- "Perhaps 2,000 monks" became "reported to number about 2,000" (the source says "it is reported").
- Polonnaruwa "copied" the design: the sign says it "more or less follows" it. Changed.
- Title said "hospitals" (plural) though only one site is documented; retitled "The monastery hospital at Mihintale".
- Pandukabhaya's "first hospitals" cut; the chronicle (Geiger's translation, with the commentary gloss "hall for the sick") only credits him with a lying-in shelter and a hall for the sick. Myth box rewritten to that claim; the unsourced "oldest hospital in the world" was dropped.
- Geiger's chronology (regnal year 58 of Pandukabhaya = 319 BCE) supports the 4th-century BCE reckoning.
- Residual doubt: both Mihintale sources are photo-catalog descriptions (the 9th-century date is the cataloger's; the 8th-century date is a modern site sign). No archaeological report was found; the date range 700–900 is wide for that reason.

## tibetan-four-tantras: corrected
- Traditional-date note and myth said tradition links the text's "arrival in Tibet" to Yuthok the Elder. No source says that; Wellcome says only that the painting shows the origins of Tibetan medicine with the 8th-century Yuthok the Elder, and LACMA calls him legendary. Reworded.
- "Which is why the book is written as questions and answers" was a causal gloss; now stated as fact of the form only. "Shown in paintings" narrowed to the one 1970 painting cited.
- Confidence note claimed scholars debate how much Yuthok the Younger composed vs. edited; nothing cited says so. Rewritten to say the sources are silent on this.
- "The tradition it codified" was softened to "the medical tradition behind it" (Kloos et al. describe the emergence of Sowa Rigpa, not the text).
- Vagbhata link note softened (the source names Indian knowledge generally, not Vagbhata).
- Image: Commons describes the central figure as the founder of the "New Medical School"; Wellcome's catalog says Yuthok the Elder. The caption says only "the physician Yuthok", which is left as is.
- Residual doubt: the date of the text rests on museum and one scholarly source; no source here discusses earlier layers.

## unani-in-india: corrected
- "Only in the late 18th century did the tradition take the name Unani" restated as "first recorded" (Aramco World says the earliest known references are late 18th century).
- "Borrowed Indian drugs" removed from the body (not in the quoted passage; the source says treatises referred to Ayurvedic practice and indigenous medicines).
- Ajmal Khan was called "founder" of the Tibbia College; the source says it was established "under the aegis of" him. Changed. Life dates for him could not be sourced and are omitted.
- Link notes: the claim that the Canon was "carried to Indian courts" and that Delhi sultans "copied" bimaristans are not in any cited source; notes softened.
- Residual doubt: the Aramco World article is a magazine piece and is internally muddled about Mughal and Sultanate dates; the 13th–18th-century range rests on general knowledge of the Delhi Sultanate (1206 onward) plus Firuz Shah's 14th-century text.

## bengal-inoculation: corrected
- Myth record said the ancient "Sanskrit account" appeared first in the *Madras Courier* in 1819. Brimnes says only that evidence of vaccination in ancient India was published there (1819), possibly as propaganda; "Sanskrit" and "first" removed.
- "Nobody has shown how old it was" restated as "its real age is undocumented".
- Shoolbred in 1805 found Holwell broadly accurate but noted modifications (cold water replaced by a warm bath); body now says so.
- Holwell's text, title page ("F.R.S.", address to the College of Physicians), Benares/Allahabad "Colleges", diet, cold water and antiquity phrase all confirmed.
- Residual doubt: the myth's "tradition" (vaccination described in ancient Sanskrit texts) is well known but not sourced to a specific popular repetition.

## indian-rhinoplasty-1794: corrected
- Body said the two Bombay surgeons "watched" Cowasjee's operation. Carpue's reprint says only that they "have seen it performed"; the Science Museum says doctors witnessed it in 1793. Now "cited as having seen the operation performed"; people note changed to match.
- Title "astonishes London" was unsupported hype; retitled. Summary "revived plastic surgery" toned to "rekindled interest in nose reconstruction" (Shaye).
- Confidence note now states exactly where sources differ: Science Museum 1793, letter implying after 1792 capture plus over a year. (Carpue also says the engraved portrait was published in 1793; the Science Museum dates its engraving 1795; not asserted in the text.)
- Confirmed: October 1794 (British Library bibliography), wax template and forehead flap, Carpue 1814/1816, cadaver practice, Sushruta describes a cheek flap. Image confirmed visually: plate labeled "Gent. Mag. Oct. 1794".
- Residual doubt: the Sushruta passage is read here as a cheek flap per the 1907 translation; other translators may read it differently.

## reserpine: substantially rewritten (myth box)
- Myth record was one-sided. The cited Somers article states that the *Sushruta Samhita* mentions a plant named Sarpagandha (identified as *Rauwolfia* centuries later), which cuts against the claim that no classical text names it. The myth record now presents the disagreement (Somers vs. Bose 1932 vs. Roy 2018) and a Somers quote was added as a fifth source.
- Body implied the 1952 isolation followed Vakil's 1949 paper as cause; now simply sequenced. "Shared Lasker honors" clarified (Kline, Vakil and the chlorpromazine pioneers each received awards that year).
- Confidence note claimed accounts differ on who isolated reserpine and in which year; the sources cited agree on 1952 CIBA (Müller, Schlittler, Bein). Replaced with the real dispute (credit to Indian researchers; antiquity of use).
- "Bazaars" is supported by Somers ("sold by quacks in the bazaars").
- Residual doubt: Somers' publication year is not shown on the PDF text (post-1957); the Sushruta identification of sarpagandha with Rauwolfia is the reviewer's assertion and is not settled.
