# Verification of batch 10a

All 9 entries re-checked against the cited sources; 26 original sources opened and quotes confirmed, 2 sources added. Validator and source checker both clean (28/28 sources).

## blood-groups: corrected
- Rh sentence said the 1940 Rh work "explained" hemolytic disease of the newborn; the Cureus source says only that it "addressed" it. Softened to "proved central to understanding".
- Image caption said "photographed around the time of his 1930 Nobel Prize"; the Commons page only says the portrait was copyrighted in 1930 and published in the NYT. Caption now says "a portrait published in 1930".
- Checked and confirmed: 1901 and the A/B/C groups, 1902 AB, Hektoen and Ottenberg 1907, citrate 1914-15, Robertson 1917 (group O, ice, 28 days), Fantus 1937 (10 days), Drew and Blood for Britain, segregation of donors, Drew's death and the debunked myth.
- Residual doubt: several people used citrate at about the same time; the body names two, and the note says so.

## hormones: corrected
- Body and summary said physiologists assumed nerves "alone" coordinated the organs. Rehfeld says blood-borne regulation was a paradigm shift as a supplement to neural regulation, not that nerves were thought the only mechanism. Softened to "largely explained".
- Secretin 1902, Hardy/Starling 1905, Uenaka's August 1900 crystallization and Parke, Davis distribution in early 1901 all confirmed in sources.
- Residual doubt: Abel's earlier "epinephrin" claim is mentioned only in the note; Otsuka says his product was impure.

## ecg: corrected
- The 1901 start of the date range had no source. Added AlGhatrif and Lindsay (PMC3714093): string galvanometer developed in 1901. Range 1901-1903 now defensible.
- "A cable" to the hospital is a telephone cable (De Coster); wording fixed.
- Waller 1887, three years of refinement, P-Q-R-S-T in 1902 and the 1924 Nobel confirmed.

## alzheimer: corrected
- Place omitted Munich. By 1906 Alzheimer worked under Kraepelin in Munich and Deter's brain was sent there (Yang et al., Nitrini). Place and body now say so.
- Everything else confirmed: Deter aged 51, November 1901, death April 1906, "peculiar substance" and "peculiar, little-known disease process" quotations, silent audience, 1907 paper, Kraepelin 1910, later abandonment of the presenile/senile split.
- Residual doubt: lecture date 3 or 6 November 1906 (Yang vs Nitrini); the note says so.

## typhoid-mary: corrected
- Summary said she was held "for most of her last 26 years". She was confined about 26 years in total (three, then 23) out of 31 years from 1907; now "held on an island for 26 years".
- The myth box and source note cited "at least 122 cases and five deaths" to Marineli; that article does not contain those numbers (it gives 22 cases in earlier households, at least 25 and two deaths at Sloane Maternity). The other copies of Soper's 1939 account are scanned and could not be checked. Replaced with the figures Marineli does give and a statement that later tallies differ.
- Confirmed: Oyster Bay 1906, 1907 capture and samples, 1910 release, 1915 return as "Mary Brown", death 1938, more than 400 other carriers none confined, disputed autopsy, press portrayal and prejudice.
- Residual doubt: total case and death counts are not firmly sourced here.

## salvarsan: corrected
- Title and summary called it "the first" magic bullet and "first targeted cure"; unearned (Ehrlich's earlier trypan red and Atoxyl work). Reworded.
- Myth record said 606 "was a laboratory number", which no cited source states. Ehrlich's Nobel biography says "the 606th of the series tested"; Aminov reads it as the sixth compound in the 600 series. The box now says the sources differ.
- Added an image: Hata's table of 606 results in mice, from Ehrlich's *Experimental Chemotherapy of Spirilloses* (Wellcome, CC BY 4.0).
- Confirmed: mercury, Bertheim and Atoxyl, Hata sent by Kitasato, 1907 shelving, 1909 rabbits, Hoechst 1910, most prescribed until penicillin, opposition, founder of chemotherapy.

## flexner-report: corrected
- Body said Flexner held up "Johns Hopkins and German universities"; Camison says Hopkins and the German and English systems. Narrowed to the German university model.
- Confirmed: 1908 AMA Council commission, 155 schools, never been in a medical school (Duffy), Pritchett's reason for choosing an educator, 120 of 162 closures (one history), "nothing conceptually new", acceleration of an existing process, only Howard and Meharry recommended, five closed schools, 27,773-35,315 estimate.
- Residual doubt: "five of the seven Black medical schools" is derived (Howard and Meharry plus the five closed schools in Campbell et al.); no source states "seven". No image: Commons search was rate-limited and nothing suitable was found.
