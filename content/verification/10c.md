# Verification report, batch 10c

Sources were re-fetched (PMC via Europe PMC XML, Nobel and FDA pages) and searched for each claim. All 27 sources pass `check-sources`; the validator reports 0 errors and 0 warnings.

## tuskegee — corrected
- Body said "yet no one proposed treating the men". Tobin says penicillin became the therapy of choice in 1944 and "there was no discussion of treating the men". Reworded to match and dated to 1944.
- Eunice Rivers "kept in touch with the men for decades" was unsupported. Tobin only shows her in 1933 and 1953 photographs working with the study's doctors. Changed to "worked alongside the study's doctors".
- The Nuremberg link note said the code was "never applied". Tobin says there is "no evidence" the study was ever discussed in its light. Reworded.
- Added an image: a public-domain CDC photo of a participant having blood drawn (undated). I avoided the widely reused "doctor injecting subject" photo.
- Residual doubt: Tobin notes many men received penicillin incidentally for other conditions, so the "untreated" label is imperfect. Ekselius gives 1929 as the start; we follow 1932.

## sulfa — corrected
- Body was over-assertive on the daughter story. It is now attributed to the Nobel biography, and her age (six) is credited to Christensen. The confidence note was reworded because the story is not in Domagk's own papers.
- "Gave the FDA new authority over drug safety" and "forced America to demand safety tests" went beyond the FDA source, which says only that the 1938 Act "increased FDA's authority to regulate drugs". Reworded.
- "Cheap molecule" was unsupported; changed to "simple". "Nazi authorities" was changed to "German authorities", the Nobel wording being "authorities of his country".
- Credit: the 1935 Pasteur Institute paper is by Tréfouël, Nitti and Bovet (Christensen's reference list). They are now named in `people`.
- Added `date.end` 1938 to match the display "1932–1938". Added an image: an FDA photo of a physician's sample bottle (I viewed it; the label has "Poison" handwritten on it).
- Residual doubt: sources do not agree on when the daughter fell ill.

## lobotomy — corrected
- "Often without a surgeon or sterile operating room" was not supported. Staudt says Freeman "did not require an anesthetist, surgeon, or even proper sterile technique". Reworded.
- The Fulton and Jacobsen report is presented as what "many have seen" as the spur to Moniz's step (Jansson's wording), not as established cause. Chimpanzee description corrected to match Jansson.
- "Almost nothing else to offer" was softened to "conventional treatments were few".
- The DBS link was "led-to", which no source supports. Changed to see-also.
- Added Moniz's life dates (Nobel page) and an image (Wellcome Collection, CC BY 4.0, Watts-Freeman instruments).
- Residual doubt: the 40,000–50,000 figure is an estimate (Coffey and Caroff).

## ect — substantially corrected
- Body said the first electrical seizure was on 11 April 1938. Gazdag says the 11 April stimulation produced no seizure; the first tonic-clonic seizure was on 20 April. Body, confidence note and quote were changed accordingly.
- "Trials show it works best for severe depression" was changed to "support ECT mainly for severe depression". The sources say depression is the first indication and give trials from the 1960s to the 1980s.
- "Demonized" was removed from the summary. Bini's life dates were unsourced and removed. Body trimmed to the tier 3 limit (under 100 words).
- Residual doubt: popular accounts say the first shock produced a seizure.

## pap-smear — corrected
- Chatterjee says Babeș "simultaneously" presented "similar findings". The note's "independent priority is well established" overstated this and was rewritten. The body now says "at about the same time".
- The summary said "doctors ignored him for a decade", which is unsupported. Dugas says he met skepticism and then "neglected this finding" for several years. Also removed "most successful cancer screen yet devised".
- "Steep falls in cervical cancer deaths" is now attributed to screening programs, per Avdulla (a "dramatic decline"). The note says no figures are given.
- Traut changed to "gynecologic pathologist" per Dugas. Added `date.end` 1943.
- Residual doubt: 1928 is a presentation date, and the sources do not agree on exactly what Babeș published when.

- The claim rating was lowered from firm to agreed. "First" depends on defining chemotherapy, and the dose date rests on one chart.

## dialysis — corrected
