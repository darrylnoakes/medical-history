# Verification — batch 09b

Second-pass check of all eight entries against the cited pages (full texts read, quotes confirmed by `check-sources.mjs`, 27/27 sources ok). No entry needed a wholesale rewrite; most problems were small overreaches.

## lister — corrected
- Body said such wounds usually ended in "amputation or death" and placed Greenlees's fracture "of the leg". The sources say only that infection was likely and amputation often required (Worboys, Cureus); none of them says which bone Greenlees broke (the Fordham text's leg-fracture boy is a different patient). Softened to "often ended in amputation" and dropped "of the leg"; the summary's "shattered leg" became "open fracture".
- People entry for Greenlees: "first case Lister reported" changed to "first case in Lister's 1867 series" (Worboys: the first article featured him).
- Image caption said the picture came from Cheyne's *Antiseptic Surgery* (1882). The Commons/Wellcome record says only "Use of the Lister carbolic spray, Antiseptic surgery, 1882" and does not name Cheyne. Caption now says what the record supports. Licence CC BY 4.0.
- Confirmed: 12 August 1865 admission; Lancet series of four weekly articles with seven further cases (11 in all) plus a fifth on abscesses; Nunneley; nine months without pyaemia, hospital gangrene or erysipelas; Anderson and Pasteur; Koch lab results on carbolic acid and the spray; Bergmann's 1890 claim (Schlich: he "claimed to have replaced antisepsis by asepsis in his hospital in the preceding two years").
- Residual doubt: the Fordham page does not itself say the paper was read at Dublin; that comes from the Cureus review (it does mention "the rules of the Association"). "Lister kept changing his materials" rests on Worboys's description of the "evolution of improvements".

## clinical-thermometer — corrected
- Wunderlich's life dates (1815–1877) had no support: the cited chapter prints 1910, which is wrong. Added a source (Open Forum Infectious Diseases, 2020) that gives 1815–1877 and the 1868 treatise.
- Confirmed: 15 years of readings, four to six a day, about 25,000 cases, 20-minute thermometers, Allbutt's 15 cm instrument made from 1867 and read in five minutes, 36.2–37.5 °C range, 36.6 °C in the 35,000-patient British study.
- Residual doubt: sources differ on Allbutt's date (one says invented 1866, manufactured 1867); the entry says "made from 1867", which is what the cited source states.

## koch — confirmed
- Checked against the Nobel biography and both PMC reviews: 24 March 1882 lecture and Virchow's skepticism, Wollstein flat and wife's microscope, earlier anthrax observers (Pollender, Rayer, Davaine), Cohn's 1876 publication, Hesses and agar in 1881, Loeffler 1883, cholera in Egypt and Calcutta (7 January 1884), Pacini 1854, vibrio isolated from healthy people, tuberculin "greatly exaggerated", Virchow's 21 autopsies (7 January 1891), diagnostic use. ## bismarck-insurance — corrected
- Body said the 1883 Act "made membership compulsory for workers in low-wage occupations". The Ridic source says Bismarck wanted funds extended to such workers but does not say "compulsory". Added Ryu (2013), who says coverage was obligatory under conditions set by law, and reworded to "obliged covered workers to join".
- Summary said the model is one "much of the world still uses"; sources support Germany and "Bismarck countries" such as Japan. Softened.
- Confirmed: hundreds of funds by 1871; message read by Bismarck on 17 November 1881; Anti-Socialist Law 1878; 4,294,173 members at end of 1885 and 9,116,332 in 1900 (GHDI table); non-profit, payroll-financed, not universal.
- Residual doubt: the Act was passed in 1883 but took effect in December 1884; sources used give only 1883. The "first national social insurance" claim is the standard secondary-source phrasing.

## asylum-era — corrected

- `date.end` was 1890 while the display reads "1840s–1880s" (and the source puts moral-treatment dominance at 1845 to the 1880s); set to 1889.
