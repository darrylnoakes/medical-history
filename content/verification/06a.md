# Verification 06a

## andean-trepanation — corrected
- Deleted the myth box. The "Inca surgeons are often said to have used coca" tradition is not a widely repeated claim; it is a speculation by the study's own lead author ("possibly coca leaves... we just don't know"), so it was not a myth. The coca link note now says "among the painkillers suggested".
- Body said smaller openings came "as surgeons learned"; sources only say the holes became smaller over time. Reworded.
- Body said treating head wounds "was probably a main purpose"; sources say head trauma was common in the highlands and multiple trepanations "may have been intended" for headaches or convulsions. Softened to "may explain" and mentioned the alternatives.
- "91 percent in some samples" changed to "a high of 91 percent" (source wording). Kushner described as lead author.
- Confirmed against sources: 800+ skulls, more than the rest of the world, earliest about 400 BCE from the southern coast, 40% / 75–83% survival, Civil War 46–56% mortality, twentieth-century reports.
- Residual doubt: survival rates are inferred from bone healing; Verano's book places the earliest "at least the fifth century BCE", so the date range is -500 to -300.
