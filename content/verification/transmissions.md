## india-into-china — substantially rewritten
- Sources did not support "monastic rules, sutras on illness and stories of the Buddha as physician were translated". Rewritten to what Giddings and Salguero show: Jivaka legend in Chinese versions of the fourth and fifth centuries (one in the Dharmaguptaka Vinaya), Sun Simiao's formulas, Dunhuang text (ninth or tenth century).
- The couching claim rested on an American Academy of Ophthalmology museum page asserting a lineage "India to China to Japan". Replaced by a peer-reviewed review (*Surv Ophthalmol* 2010, PMC2916044): monks brought couching at the end of the Western Han; clearest documentation is Tang (Wang Tao, 752). Japan dropped. The review's Han date sits oddly with the usual dating of Buddhism's arrival, so the note now flags it as a single account.
- Dates changed from c. 250–900 to c. 300–900: nothing sourced supports 250; the earliest evidence is fourth-century.
- Residual doubt: Jivaka's skull-opening was removed because the cited passage attributes it to the Sanskrit biography, not the Chinese one. Wider influence on Chinese medicine remains unshown.
