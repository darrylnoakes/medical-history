## montagu-variolation — corrected
- Body said the April 1721 inoculation was "the first such operation in Britain" flatly; the only source is the Liverpool piece's "the first time the procedure was performed in Britain". Changed to "reported as the first".
- Myth "tradition" claimed she "discovered" inoculation, a strawman; reworded to the real popular claim (single-handedly brought it to Britain after learning it from Turkish women). The record side is sourced (Timoni 1714, Maitland performed the operations).
- Link note to Onesimus said "the same year, Boston learned inoculation"; softened (Boston's 1721 campaign drew on his account).
- Image caption said the son was "the first of her children to be engrafted" under a c. 1717 painting; reworded. Commons file exists, public domain, described as c. 1717 NPG portrait with her son and attendants.

- Arctic 1875–76: body said lime juice "failed". Deacon says lime juice was less effective and that some ship victims had kept taking it, but the inquiry blamed omission on the sledges. Reworded to say what the source supports.
- Myth record said "further evidence gathered at sea"; now names the 1793 Madras voyage (Tröhler). "Heat-treated concentrate" changed to "boiled-down concentrate with almost no vitamin C" (Milne).
- Vitamins link note "160 years later" fixed to "well over a century later".
- Confirmed: date, twelve men, six remedies, six days of fruit, four of 450 pages, 1754 Sick and Hurt Board refusal, 1795 Blane, 1815 near-disappearance.
- Residual doubt: fabrication allegation (Baron) is unresolved; the paste's full ingredients (balsam of Peru, myrrh) are abbreviated. Image (Wellcome title page, CC BY) is fine.
