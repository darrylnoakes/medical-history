## ibn-zuhr: corrected
- Body said the goat piece was "the size of a lupine seed". Abdel-Halim's translation says "just less than the size of a tirmisah (lupine seed)". Changed to "just smaller than".
- "Praised by Ibn Rushd" was vague. TDV quotes Ibn Rushd calling the *Taysir* the most perfect work of experimental medicine of his time, and says he was about 30 years younger than Ibn Zuhr. Body and people line now say this. "Friend" was dropped, because TDV says only "close relationship".
- Summary said he tried the goat "to settle" the question, which is Abdel-Halim's reading. It now says "by his own account" and "having read the quarrel".
- The confidence note said "one source" gives 1121–1162. It is Al-Rubaye. The note also now says the experiment dates from his training, earlier than the book, and rests on his own report alone.
- TDV supports text now covers the dedication to Abd al-Mu'min (whose vizier he became).
- Doubt: the date is a book date (Taysir, 1121–1162), not an experiment date.

## maimonides: corrected
- Body said the regimen rested on "diet, exercise and clean air" with no date. Added a source (Rosner 2010, PMC3721660) that dates the Regimen of Health to 1198, written for al-Afdal. Wording is now "stresses moderate diet, exercise and clean air".
- Myth box: the record presented a negative ("popular phrase compresses this") as settled. The Cureus review (PMC11565266) calls him "private physician of King Saladin". Rosner says he was court physician to the vizier. Record rewritten to say some modern writers state it flatly, that the sources used here name al-Fadil and al-Afdal, and that none records him treating Saladin himself.
- Link note to Ibn Zuhr ("drew on the physicians of al-Andalus") was unsupported. Rosner says Ibn Zuhr was among the Muslim authors he quoted often. Note changed.
- Doubt: Rosner dates his appointment as court physician to 1174, far earlier than the "last two decades" of medical writing. Nobody dates the writings that early, so the range 1185–1204 stands. Birth year 1135 or 1138 is disputed.

## ibn-al-nafis: corrected (image added)
- Body said the blood "mixes with air" in the lungs. None of the cited sources says this (West quotes the blood being "heated" and passing to the left ventricle). Cut.
- Servetus paragraph: TDV says Alpago's translator knew of the discovery, but the body had left that out. Now stated as one encyclopedia's view, against the many historians who doubt it.
- Myth record said "No Latin version of his passage on the lungs is known". That is a negative the sources do not prove. West says the 1547 Alpago translation, on compound drugs, "apparently" did not mention the lungs. Reworded to match.
- The confidence note now spells out the date range (arrival in Cairo in 1236 to death in 1288). The anchor of age 29 depends on an uncertain birth year (1210 or 1213).
- Tier 2 had no image. Added the illuminated opening page of a manuscript of a commentary by Ibn al-Nafis on the *Canon* (Wellcome, CC BY 4.0). Checked the file page and viewed the image. The Commons description says only "Commentary on Avicenna's Canon", so the caption says the catalog does not identify which commentary or the date.
- Doubt: it is unclear that the lung passage appears on this page. Chronology is odd, because West has him physician-in-chief at the Mansuri hospital, which opened in 1284, long after the commentary.

## mansuri-hospital: corrected
- Summary said the hospital was "endowed in 1284" and "paid patients" to go home. Northrup dates the founding to 683/1284. The founding deeds she cites are dated April 1286, and 683 AH runs into 1285. Summary and note now say "founded", and the note carries the deed date. The money and clothing were given at discharge.
- Body ran together the staff, wards and clothing details. Rewritten with al-Maqrizi attributed (Khitat, as quoted by the Qalawun VR project).
- "Treatment and employment were limited to Muslims" was broader than Northrup, who says Christians and Jews were excluded. Now says "Christians and Jews were barred". The myth record is attributed to Northrup, with her caveat "appears to be a new development" and the note that Christian and Jewish doctors still served at court.
- "Decayed by 1800" is now "disrepair by the French expedition of 1798". The eye clinic is dated by the 1912 building and the 1915 reopening.
- Doubt: the daily-life details depend on later chroniclers. The Qalawun VR project is a university-linked site, not a peer-reviewed paper.

- Body said both men had to square contagion with the "no contagion" hadith. Only Ibn Khatima's treatise is shown doing this (his last four of ten questions). Nothing cited says it of Ibn al-Khatib. Restricted to Ibn Khatima.

- Date range's upper limit of 1374 is only Ibn al-Khatib's death. The note now says so.
