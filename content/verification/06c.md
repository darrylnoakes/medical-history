# Verification: batch 06c

Sources were re-fetched and read by the second-pass checker. Where pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov served a CAPTCHA, the article was read through the NCBI efetch API. All 28 sources now pass the machine check.

## universities-medicine — corrected
- Body said Bologna's university "grew out of student guilds" and that medicine was a founding subject of the first universities. Osler and Pace say Bologna's guilds were of law students. Reworded so that medicine is "among the subjects taught" and Bologna's guilds are "at first of law".
- Body implied Mondino's manual was used at Montpellier. Pace says it was a guide in the universities' medical course generally, alongside Henri de Mondeville's text. Reworded.
- Jacoba Felicie's "about thirty" is her own pleading's description of her ("triginta annorum vel citra", judged by her appearance), not an established age. Now says so.
- "The same order named other healers" changed to "the same prohibition was served on" others. The Chartularium names Stephen the Burgundian, Jacques Lepele, Johanna and others besides Margaret and Belota.
- Summary said the doctors used licenses "to drive out rivals", which is interpretive. Replaced with a neutral statement. It now says her patients praised her, which matches the witness testimony.
- Confidence note now says the Montpellier statutes and bull rest on later reference works.
- Added image: Laurentius de Voltolina, a Bologna lecture scene. Commons metadata confirms the description, the date (second half of the 14th century), the Berlin Kupferstichkabinett and public-domain status. It shows a university lecture, not a medical one specifically.
- Residual doubt: the date range (1180–1289) does not cover the 1309 and 1322 episodes in the body. The 1180 lower bound is a loose choice, and Montpellier had physicians as early as 1137.

## spectacles — substantially rewritten
- Body said the chronicle credits Alessandro with making glasses "around 1290". It does not. The Dizionario Biografico reads Giordano's 1306 sermon as giving c. 1290. The chronicle only says Alessandro copied an earlier unnamed maker. Fixed.
- Body said "most likely in Pisa" as if settled. That is Rasmussen, a chemistry-history paper. The Enciclopedia Italiana, following Albertotti, says "with all probability Venice" and notes a widely accepted view of an unknown Venetian glassmaker, citing guild rules from the end of the 13th century. Both views are now stated, and place is "Pisa or Venice".
- Date anchor c. 1286 had no direct support except Rasmussen's "shortly after 1286". Changed to c. 1290 with range 1285–1300, matching the sources.
- Removed Giordano's death date (1311), which no source states. Alessandro is now "d. by 1313", which is what the sources say.
- Myth: "first appears in del Migliore 1684" was an inference. It now says the epitaph is known only from del Migliore, who cited an old burial register. Added del Lungo's 1920 study and the Enciclopedia's wording. Added the second Treccani quote as its own source entry.
- Link to ibn-al-haytham-optics changed from built-on to see-also. No source shows spectacles drew on Ibn al-Haytham, and the Enciclopedia treats Bacon only as a precursor.
- Summary shortened and the claimant described as "a forgery" instead of a "hoax on a tomb".
- Residual doubt: the date range is genuinely loose. The sermon's own wording was not seen in a source that could be opened. A 1913 BMJ address on the topic was scan-only.
- No image added: Commons search was rate-limited.

## mondino — corrected
- Confidence note said the Anathomia is "dated 1316 in its own text". No source states this. Now says sources agree on 1316.
- Body said "dissections almost every month" on criminals and the poor. Treccani also mentions pigs. Added.
- Body said the book "was written to be read aloud". Encyclopedia.com says this is "likely". Softened to "style suggests".
- The reader/demonstrator/dissector scene is dated by Encyclopedia.com to illustrations of the later 14th century. Body no longer says "printed editions".
- Birth date: sources give c. 1275 (Unibo) and c. 1270 (DBI), so people now shows a range.
- Myth: "first female anatomist" is unsourced, so reworded to a "pioneering female anatomist". "Woodcut" changed to "image in a 15th-century edition", the wording of the Unibo page. "Made up" changed to "passed off", as Unibo says.
- Summary no longer asserts schools read the manual aloud over corpses.
- Link to alexandria-dissection said "the last systematic human dissection, some 1,500 years earlier". That overclaims ("last") and misstates the interval. Reworded.
- Residual doubt: whether Mondino cut himself is open. The unibo page's claim that he was the first to pair dissection with classical texts is not repeated.

## black-death — corrected
- Body said plague DNA came "from the teeth of seven people". In fact seven teeth were sampled, three showed evidence of plague, and two genomes were reconstructed. Fixed, along with the confidence note.
- "Exact root of the branching" is not Spyrou's wording. Now "most recent common ancestor of the major diversification", with Tian Shan comparison supporting local emergence. The marmot and rodent claim was dropped.
- Body said DNA and proteins were found at plague pits in England, France, Germany, Italy and the Netherlands (Haensch 2010). In fact aDNA came from England, France and the Netherlands only. The Italian and German pits (Parma, Augsburg) were PCR-negative and positive only for the F1 antigen. Fixed. Also removed the unsupported claim that medieval accounts "did not match modern outbreaks".
- "Golden Horde's ports" changed to "territories" (Max Planck wording). The Paris faculty report was written "at the command of" King Philip, not "asked by".
- Guy's "rendered doctors useless" had no source in this entry. Added the Northeastern translation as a source.
- "The lasting responses would be administrative rather than medical" was unsourced and too absolute. Softened to "some of the lasting responses, such as quarantine".
- Summary no longer says DNA "traced" the source to tombstones.
- Checked and kept: Strasbourg burning, Königshofen dates, and the Carlin Paris report. The existing image (Tournai burial) was confirmed on Commons: public domain, Gilles li Muisis, KBR MS 13076-77.
- Residual doubt: the Central Asian origin rests on one 2022 study. The 30–60% toll is an estimate.
