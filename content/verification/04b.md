# Verification: batch 04b (East Asia)

## sun-simiao: confirmed
All sources open and quotes match. Checked against Yan Liu: 650s date, thirty scrolls selected for emergencies, self-taught after a sickly youth, 581?-682. Fruehauf's translation supports the reading list (classics, histories, Laozi, divination) and 652. The link note (Sun's Supplement copied drug descriptions from the state materia medica) is supported by Liu, ch. 4. No changes. Residual doubt: 652 is the conventional date; Liu says only "650s".

## tang-pharmacopoeia: corrected
- Body said "twenty-odd officials, twelve of them court medical specialists"; Liu gives 22 officials, 12 from the medical/drug offices. Made precise (ChinaCulture says 23; confidence note already records this).
- Confidence note said "Chinese popular sources" call it the world's first pharmacopoeia; the source is a Ministry of Culture site. Reworded, and the `supports` text for that source now says the claim is the site's own.
- 659 (17 January), 850 substances, 114 new, the 669 Dunhuang copy and the 731 Japanese copy all confirmed. Residual doubt: scroll count 54 or 55 across sources.

## bronze-acupuncture-man: corrected
- Body said the figure "served as the first national standard" in the entry's own voice and said the manual was made "alongside" the figure. The first is the HKBU museum's claim, now attributed. The Wellcome source documents only a 1443 woodcut edition of Wang's manual, so the body now says that and no longer implies it was published with the figure in 1027.
- "Points had worn illegible" is in the museum text ("unrecognizable"); wording matched.
- Residual doubt: the wax-and-water examination is not documented in any Song-period text among the sources; it is hedged as "accounts say". The date is 1027 per HKBU and the Tiansheng reign (1023-31) per ChinaCulture. Sources describe one statue (other scholarship mentions two); the entry follows the sources.

## song-ci-forensics: corrected
- "Earliest known systematic guide" was stated flatly. The only support is Chinese government and review sources, and the claim has not been tested against earlier texts. Body, summary and confidence note now attribute it ("ranked in Chinese accounts as the earliest of its kind").
- Added a PMC source (Forensic Sciences Research, 2016) showing a scholarly source dates the book to 1248 and gives Song Ci as 1186-1249. The note now says dates differ (1247 vs 1248); the 1245-1249 range already covers it.
- Replaced the Europe PMC XML URL with the pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov URL.
- Confirmed: Fujian, four terms as senior judge, contents, spread to Korea/Japan/Vietnam, dozens of later manuals, lividity and moved bodies. Sickle story remains omitted (unsourced).

## chinese-variolation: corrected
- Summary said "nearly two centuries before inoculation reached Europe"; 1549 to the early 1700s is about 170 years. Changed to "well over a century".
- Added `date.traditional` (c. 1000 CE) for the monk/nun tradition, sourced to History of Vaccines (Needham quote).
- Myth box: "hermit physician" is not in the source; the CDC article says "Mount Emei physician" and "son of a Song prime minister". Fixed wording. The record now notes the Needham finding for the monk/nun version.
- Confidence note said whether China or India came first "remains open". The CDC review says the earliest detailed records known are Chinese, though the date and place of origin are uncertain, and that India's definitive written records date mainly from the 18th century. Reworded.
- 1465-1572 range, Jiangxi, Taiping County (Longqing), the four methods, donor selection, repeated passage, and Kangxi sending Jiangxi inoculators to Mongolia all confirmed in the CDC article. 1549 for the Douzhen xinfa is only "believed to be the oldest record" (Hwang); the body already says "usually cited".
- No suitable image on Commons (only 18th-century pediatric pox plates, or cowpox prints); none added.

## bencao-gangmu: corrected
- Body said Li died in 1593 "around the time it appeared". No source says that. Rewritten: engraved at Nanjing in 1590 (Wellcome), Li died 1593, one study calls it posthumous, publication dates range 1590-1596.
- Confidence note no longer asserts an explanation as fact ("may reflect").
- Link note for artemisinin was a vague claim; reworded to Tu Youyou's team searching the classical drug literature.
- Confirmed: 1578 at age 60, Qizhou, physician family, 27 years, 1,892 substances, 11,096 prescriptions, 1,109 illustrations, ten subheadings, 1,600-year history, ~1.6 million characters (Unschuld) vs ~2 million (Frontiers), Linnaeus parallel "functional and heuristic." Image file checked on Commons: it is the Wellcome photograph of the first page of the Jinling edition; caption matches.
- Residual doubt: Frontiers describes an "eight-section" drug format; Unschuld says up to ten subheadings. The body follows Unschuld and says "up to ten".
