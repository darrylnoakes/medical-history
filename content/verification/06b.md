All 39 quotes were re-checked by script and by reading each cited page. Changes are listed per entry.

## calabar-bean — corrected (minor)
- Quotes, 1855 self-experiment, 1863 Fraser description, 1864 isolation (Jobst and Hesse), Laqueur 1876, Julian 1935 all confirmed in the cited pages.
- People line and summary called Christison an Edinburgh "professor"; no source says so (Proudfoot: "a toxicologist of repute"). Changed to "toxicologist"; summary now says "an Edinburgh toxicologist" and "milestone in synthetic chemistry" instead of the evaluative "landmark of chemistry".
- Doubt: life dates for Christison and Fraser are not given (unsourced). The "glaucoma use from 1876" rests on a bibliography entry and the AAO/EyeWiki history, not on a clinical paper.

## bunyoro-caesarean — corrected
- Banana wine, cautery, seven iron pins, 11 days, Kahura and 1884 publication all confirmed (Science Museum, Hektoen, PAMJ).
- Body said the technique was "practiced" and the report "remains the only eyewitness account"; confidence note said "no other witness or local record describes it". No source says either. ## monastic-medicine — corrected
- Body gave Benedict's death as 543 flatly. The cited Catholic Encyclopedia says 543 (Totila's visit) is the only certain date in his life and that death in that year is only one writer's view. Changed to "about 543"; source `supports` updated.
- Rule chapter 36 supports separate room, attendant, baths and meat (meat only for the very weak; body simplified but not contradicted). The OSB page is Doyle's translation adapted for a women's community (Abbess, sisters); the quoted sentence is identical in the original, and the source title now says so.
- Cassiodorus: the passage tells readers to read Dioscorides's herbal and then Hippocrates and Galen "translated into Latin"; body implied all three in Latin. Fixed. Institutions date 543–555 comes from a 1908 reference; body now says "540s or 550s".
- Link to arabic-into-latin changed from led-to to see-also (monastic care did not cause the translations).
- Doubt: Benedict's and the Rule's dates are traditional; Cassiodorus's Institutions are probably dated later by modern specialists.

## arabic-into-latin — corrected (minor)
- Confirmed: arrival c. 1076, death before 1098/99, Ifriqiya origin, two languages and editor, Pantegni/al-Majusi, Stephen of Antioch in the 1120s, 400-year curriculum, Toledo 1085, Gerard by 1144, 68 (NLM) to about 80 (MacTutor) works, Gerard as translator of Rhazes and Avicenna (Catholic Encyclopedia), Latin Canon to the 17th century, translation lull 1300–1480.
- Plagiarism claim: Green says the main accusation comes from one person, Stephen, and that Constantine's contemporaries did not think him the original author. Body now says "the main charge of plagiarism comes from Stephen of Antioch" instead of presenting it as general.
- Myth record said "modern scholars call over-the-top"; the source is one scholar (Green) on the Cassinese biography. Reworded.
- Image checked (Strasbourg Canon, before 1473, hand decoration): matches.
- Doubt: "first person known" to translate a major body of Arabic medicine is Green's claim; the SEP says Constantine "tried to disguise" origins, while Green says he never hid his African origin (different matters). Gerard's story of going to Toledo to learn Arabic is from a MacTutor biography.
