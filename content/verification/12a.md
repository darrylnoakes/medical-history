# Verification: batch 12a

## ebola-1976 (corrected)
- Body gave Nzara 151 deaths of 284 patients. Languon & Quaye's table gives 284 cases and 151 deaths for the whole Sudan outbreak, which ran through four towns (Nzara, Maridi, Tembura, Juba). Rewritten so the figures belong to the outbreak, not to Nzara.
- "Cotton-factory town" overstated. The source says Nzara was reported as the source and that the factory is suspected (it also notes no evidence tied it to the outbreak in 1979). Now "suspected". Summary changed from "Sudanese factory town" to "southern Sudan". Confidence note updated.
- "Evacuated nun" changed to "a nun's blood" (the interview says Muyembe took her to Kinshasa and sent a blood sample). Bare-handed sampling confirmed in the interview. Other numbers (318/280, five syringes) confirmed. Body trimmed to the tier-3 limit.
- Residual doubt: none material.

## smallpox-eradication (corrected)
- People list and body called Janet Parker "the last person to die"; the cited source says "last person to contract" smallpox. Reworded to match. Her route of infection is "conjecture" in the source, not "never settled"; reworded.
- "Two official repositories" now matches WHO text (two designated laboratories, US and Russia).
- Myth record said the Leicester method protected contacts; the source describes notification, isolation and quarantine, with contact vaccination added later. Fixed; the 1946 Libya expanding-ring precedent is as sourced.
- Dates, 300 million deaths, Foege, 150,000 workers in India, last cases (Banu 1975, Maalin 26 Oct 1977), 9 Dec 1979 and May 1980 all confirmed. Image file page matches the caption.
- Residual doubt: the "ring vaccination was brand new" myth is a modest popular idea.

## ivf (corrected)
- Birth "in Oldham": none of the cited sources states where Louise was delivered. Reworded to "after IVF treatment carried out at Oldham" and the place field now says where the treatment was done. A source naming the delivery hospital would be needed to restore the stronger wording.
- "Went to Edwards alone" implied a cause the sources do not give; now states only that he was the one of the three still alive.
- Myth "conceived in a test tube by two men" was contrived; reduced to the real "test-tube baby" tradition, with the Nobel committee's own note that it was a culture dish.
- Confirmed: nine years of trying, eight-cell embryo, 1969 fertilization, MRC refusal, private donation, Steptoe's death 1988, four million births, Purdy's role, 1980-81 plaque dispute, her death in 1985, Durga on 3 Oct 1978, committee, 1981 suicide, Anand Kumar 1997.
- No image: Commons search was rate-limited, and nothing suitable was verified.
- Residual doubt: Mukhopadhyay's claim rests on later reviews of his own reports.

## alma-ata (corrected)
- Summary said donors offered a "cheaper" plan; sources say cost-effective and more realistic. Reworded. "In place of community-led care" softened to the source's "shift away from the community emphasis". Conference dates (6-12 Sept 1978), Bellagio 1979 and participants confirmed.

## hiv-aids (corrected)
- The "citizen scientists" quote describes early activists generally, not ACT UP alone; reworded. WHO's 44.2 million is a cumulative "to date" figure; only the 41 million living figure is dated end of 2025. Fixed.
- Confirmed: MMWR 5 June 1981 (five cases, three LA hospitals), 355 cases by May 1982, 1983 isolation, April 1984 Gallo announcement, 1920 (1909-1930) Kinshasa, 1959-1960 samples, Haiti mid-1960s, US 1969 (Worobey via a review). Image caption matches the Commons description.
- Residual doubt: the 1969 US arrival is a single estimate relayed by a secondary source.

Softened; claim confidence lowered from firm to agreed. Source supports fields corrected (Millman, 1986 recombinant confirmed in the Italy review).
