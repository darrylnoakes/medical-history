# Verification, batch 01a

## shanidar-care: corrected
- Date display said "c. 50,000–45,000 years ago"; the Pomeroy et al. source gives 46–50 ka (PLOS says about 50 ka). Display changed to "c. 50,000–46,000 years ago" and `latest` to 44000 BCE.
- Confidence note now says the radiocarbon date (1950s, charcoal near the skeleton) "needs to be regarded cautiously" although later redating broadly supports it (Pomeroy et al.), instead of implying it is solid.
- "survived only with help" softened to "must have relied on his group", the wording of the WashU release and PLOS paper.
- Checked and confirmed: crushed left orbit, probable above-elbow amputation, abnormal gait, ear-canal exostoses, age 40–50, discovery by Solecki in 1957, and the primate-based doubts about the care inference.
- Residual doubt: "probable deafness" is the authors' inference from the exostoses, not an observation.

## borneo-amputation: corrected
- Confidence note said the tooth (US-ESR) dating showed "at least 31,000 years". It actually returned 25.4 ± 4.3 ka (1σ), which the authors say is within error of the radiocarbon dates. Charcoal dates are 31.1–30.4 cal ka and the Bayesian model gives 31.2–30.7 ka. Note rewritten; `latest` set to 28400 BCE.
- Body said "a child lost" the leg and that the person "lived another six to nine years". The paper says "probably as a child" and that 6–9 years is a minimum (death at about 19–20). Changed to "probably as a child", "at least six to nine years", and added the age at death.
- Body said "only a deliberate operation could leave such a stump" as if it were the team's proof. Reworded to what the team argues.
- "A group of practicing orthopaedic surgeons" was not backed by any cited source. Added Murphy et al. (Nature 2023) as a source (authors are in a Department of Traumatology and write as "orthopaedic trauma surgeons practicing in this field") and described their argument (growth-plate fracture) accurately.
- Maloney changed from "led the excavation" to "co-led" (Sydney release: led by Aubert and Maloney, with Dilkes-Hall and Priyatno). "Foragers must have known anatomy" now attributed to the authors.
- Residual doubt: the diagnosis is genuinely contested; claim rating `disputed` kept. No image: nothing suitable found on Commons.

## trepanation: corrected
- Body put the larger Ensisheim opening "on top". The Archaeology source says the first was "toward the front" and Escolà says both lie on the midline; the position claim was dropped.
- "Dnieper Rapids" was not in any cited source; removed from body and `place` (Vasilyevka II, Ukraine is sourced by Lillie and Escolà). People line for Lillie adjusted.
- "Many patients across Neolithic Europe survived, sometimes for years" overreached. Escolà's survey covers Alsace: most survived, one for several years. Rewritten accordingly.
- Date note: grave goods give 5100–4900 BCE with radiocarbon corroboration (Archaeology 1997). The `earliest` of 7000 BCE is a loose allowance for the Mesolithic cases, whose dates the sources do not give; the note now says so.
- Myth box kept: the "evil spirits" story is widely repeated and the Kisii analogy is sourced.
- Image: Wellcome description matches the caption (Prunières collection, Jardin des Plantes museum, survival indicated). Rate-limited, so the licence was not re-queried; the writer's saved file page shows the Wellcome Collection source.
- Residual doubt: the Lillie page is paywalled; the quote is from its abstract (nature.com sometimes returns 503 to the checker).
