## ether-anaesthesia — corrected
- Summary said operating rooms "around the world fell quiet"; replaced with the sourced claim that ether was in use in many countries within a year (Firth 2022).
- Body said Morton "campaigned for decades"; Firth says only that he campaigned after his patent was ruled unenforceable. Reworded.
- Body said Wells "had a tooth pulled"; it was his own tooth (11 December 1844, per Mistry et al.). Clarified.
- "Easier to give than ether but could kill without warning" was unsupported. Replaced with McKenzie's detail that Simpson used no special apparatus, only a cloth, and that Hannah Greener died on 28 January 1848.
- Myth box called the 1859 book "Morton's promotional biography". Firth says it was an authorized biography written to support his claim; reworded, and the overstated "no earlier evidence supports it" dropped in favor of the dates.
- Slow adoption in Boston: Firth says caution about dangers "may have contributed"; the text now hedges.
- Link to `hanaoka-seishu` changed from `built-on` to `see-also` (his work was unknown in the West, so nothing was built on it). Lister link note softened.
- Image: Commons file is the NMAH exhibit photo (CC0, Daderot); caption matches the file title.
- Residual doubt: Firth gives January 1846 for Wells's Boston demonstration, which is evidently a slip for 1845; the body gives no date for it.

## snow-cholera — corrected
- Brewery: body said the workmen "drank beer and the brewery's own well water". Snow's text (quoted by Tulchinsky) says they drank no water at all and the brewery had a deep well. Fixed.
- Pacini: "largely ignored until Koch found the organism again in 1884" was not supported. Doni et al. say his discovery went unrecognized in his lifetime and the name *Vibrio cholerae* Pacini 1854 was adopted only in 1965; Koch's 7 January 1884 isolation is also from that source. Rewritten.
- "Second edition" of Snow's book removed (no source gives the edition). "Sharp detective" trimmed. Confidence note no longer cites a "parish inquiry" that is not among the sources.
- Image caption: dropped "first exhibited in December 1854" (Commons says only "1854").
- Confirmed: 31 August night start, 500 fatal attacks in 250 yards, 5 of 535 workhouse deaths, handle removed the next day, outbreak already declining, 315 versus 37 deaths per 10,000 houses.
- Residual doubt: the myth ("Snow himself removed the handle") is common in popular tellings but the record text rests on Tulchinsky's phrasing that he "persuaded" the authorities.
